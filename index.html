Our Mission

Building Understanding, One Step at a Time

A structured path to authentic Islamic knowledge following the methodology of the righteous predecessors.

The Hanbali Knowledge Project is dedicated to providing authentic Islamic education based on the Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah, according to the understanding of the righteous predecessors (Salaf as-Salih).

We follow the Hanbali school of jurisprudence (Fiqh), the creed of Ahl as-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah, and emphasize the importance of studying under qualified teachers with proper chains of narration.

Our curriculum is designed for gradual progression — from foundational knowledge to advanced studies — ensuring that every student builds a solid understanding before moving forward.

The Inheritors of the Prophets

Great Scholars

"The scholars are the inheritors of the Prophets." — Authentic Hadith

Biographies

Scholars of Ahl as-Sunnah

Learn about the lives, contributions, and works of the scholars who preserved authentic Islamic knowledge.

أ

Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal

Founder of the Hanbali School (164-241 AH)

One of the four great Imams of Islamic jurisprudence and a towering figure in hadith scholarship. He endured severe persecution under the Mihna for refusing to affirm the createdness of the Quran. His Musnad contains ~30,000 hadith.

Key Works:

Musnad Ahmad, Kitab as-Sunnah, Ar-Radd 'ala al-Jahmiyyah

ت

Ibn Taymiyyah

Shaykh al-Islam (661-728 AH)

A prolific scholar of unmatched breadth in Aqeedah, Fiqh, Hadith, and Tafsir. He dedicated his life to reviving the methodology of the Salaf and refuting innovations. Produced hundreds of works.

Key Works:

Al-Aqeedah al-Wasitiyyah, Al-Fatawa al-Kubra, Minhaj as-Sunnah

ق

Ibn al-Qayyim

The Student of Ibn Taymiyyah (691-751 AH)

A brilliant scholar, poet, and physician who was the closest student of Ibn Taymiyyah. His works uniquely combine deep knowledge with spiritual insight. Most famous work: Madarij as-Salikin.

Key Works:

Madarij as-Salikin, Zad al-Ma'ad, Al-Fawaid

ب

Sheikh Ibn Baz

Grand Mufti of Saudi Arabia (1330-1420 AH)

Former Grand Mufti and member of the Council of Senior Scholars. Known for vast knowledge, humility, and dedication to teaching. Issued thousands of fatawa and authored numerous books.

Key Works:

Majmu' Fatawa, Sharh Bulugh al-Maram, Tawhid treatises

أ

Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani

The Muhaddith of the Era (1332-1420 AH)

The preeminent hadith scholar of the 20th century. Dedicated his life to distinguishing authentic from weak narrations. Produced over 100 works on hadith and its sciences.

Key Works:

Silsilah as-Saheehah, Silsilah ad-Da'ifah, Sahih at-Targhib

ف

Salih al-Fawzan

Member of the Council of Senior Scholars (b. 1354 AH)

Member of the Council of Senior Scholars in Saudi Arabia. Leading scholar of Aqeedah and Fiqh. Regularly teaches at the Grand Mosque in Makkah. His explanations of classical texts are widely studied.

Key Works:

Aqeedah at-Tawhid, Sharh al-Aqeedah al-Wasitiyyah, Fatawa

The Four Caliphs

The successors of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) who led the Muslim Ummah with justice.

1
أ

Abu Bakr as-Siddiq

The First Caliph (573-634 CE)

Abdullah ibn Uthman, known as Abu Bakr as-Siddiq (The Truthful), was the closest companion of the Prophet. He was the first adult male to accept Islam and remained the Prophet's most steadfast supporter.

During his caliphate (632-634 CE), he successfully suppressed the Ridda Wars and initiated the compilation of the Quran. Known for extreme generosity. The Prophet said: "If I were to take a Khalil, I would have taken Abu Bakr."

Key Contributions:

Compilation of the Quran, Unification of Arabia, Establishment of the Caliphate

2
ع

Umar ibn al-Khattab

The Second Caliph (584-644 CE)

Umar ibn al-Khattab, known as al-Faruq (The One Who Distinguishes Between Truth and Falsehood), was a towering figure whose conversion strengthened the Muslim community immensely.

During his caliphate (634-644 CE), the Islamic empire expanded to include Persia, Egypt, and the Levant. He established the Islamic calendar (Hijri), introduced the Diwan, and founded Basra and Kufa. The Prophet said: "If there were to be a Prophet after me, it would be Umar."

Key Contributions:

Islamic calendar, Administrative reforms, Expansion of the Caliphate

3
ع

Uthman ibn Affan

The Third Caliph (576-656 CE)

Uthman ibn Affan, known as Dhun-Nurayn (The Possessor of Two Lights) because he married two of the Prophet's daughters, was known for his modesty, generosity, and deep love for the Quran.

During his caliphate (644-656 CE), he oversaw the standardization of the Quran into one official copy (Mushaf Uthmani). He was martyred while reading the Quran. The Prophet said: "Every Prophet has a companion, and my companion in Paradise is Uthman."

Key Contributions:

Standardization of the Quran (Mushaf Uthmani), Naval expansion

4
ع

Ali ibn Abi Talib

The Fourth Caliph (601-661 CE)

Ali ibn Abi Talib, the cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet, was the first child to accept Islam. He was known as the "Gate of Knowledge" for his profound understanding of the Quran and Sunnah.

During his caliphate (656-661 CE), he faced immense challenges including civil strife. He ruled with justice and wisdom, leaving behind a legacy of knowledge, bravery, and piety. He was martyred while praying Fajr. The Prophet said: "I am the city of knowledge and Ali is its gate."

Key Contributions:

Vast knowledge transmission, Judicial wisdom, Military leadership

About Us

The Hanbali Knowledge Project

Seeking authentic Islamic knowledge upon the Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah, according to the understanding of the Salaf as-Salih.

Our Mission

The Hanbali Knowledge Project was founded with a clear purpose: to make authentic Islamic knowledge accessible to everyone who sincerely seeks it. In an age of confusion and misinformation, we believe it is essential to return to the sources of divine guidance.

We follow the methodology of Ahl as-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah, adhering to the understanding of the righteous predecessors (Salaf as-Salih). Our approach is rooted in the Hanbali school of jurisprudence.

Our curriculum is designed for gradual, structured learning. We believe knowledge must be built step by step, like constructing a building.

Our Methodology

We adhere to the following principles:

  • Quran First: The Quran is the primary source of all Islamic knowledge.
  • Authentic Sunnah: We rely only on authentic hadith, verified through rigorous sciences.
  • Understanding of the Salaf: We interpret according to the Companions and early generations.
  • Hanbali Fiqh: We teach jurisprudence according to the madhhab of Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal.
  • Scholarly Referencing: All content is based on established classical and contemporary works.
  • Gradual Progression: Knowledge is presented in a structured manner for all levels.

About the Founder

This project was established by Ahmed Maqsood Alli, a student of knowledge dedicated to compiling and organizing Islamic educational resources for English-speaking Muslims.
